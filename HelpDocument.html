Frequently asked questions

Brush your teeth at least twice a day, preferably after meals, using fluoride toothpaste.

Yes, flossing helps remove plaque and food particles between teeth that brushing alone can't reach. Aim to floss at least once a day.

Bad breath can be caused by poor oral hygiene, bacteria in the mouth, dry mouth, or certain foods. To prevent it, brush your teeth and tongue regularly, use mouthwash, stay hydrated, and avoid foods like garlic and onions.

It's recommended to visit the dentist every six months for regular check-ups and cleanings. However, your dentist may suggest more frequent visits based on your oral health needs.

Signs of gum disease include red, swollen, or bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, and loose teeth. To prevent gum disease, brush and floss daily, eat a balanced diet, avoid tobacco, and visit your dentist regularly.

Prevent tooth decay by brushing and flossing regularly, using fluoride toothpaste, limiting sugary snacks and drinks, and visiting your dentist for fluoride treatments and sealants.

If you have a toothache, rinse your mouth with warm saltwater, use dental floss to remove any trapped food, and take over-the-counter pain relievers. Contact your dentist if the pain persists.

You can whiten your teeth safely by using whitening toothpaste, whitening strips, or professional whitening treatments recommended by your dentist.

In case of a dental emergency such as a knocked-out tooth, severe toothache, or broken tooth, contact your dentist immediately for guidance and treatment.

Electric toothbrushes can be more effective at removing plaque and reducing gingivitis compared to manual brushes. However, both can be effective if used correctly with proper technique.
